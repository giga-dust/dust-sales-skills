# Enterprise Sales Motion

Help the user transition from founder-led (or ad-hoc) sales to a repeatable, scalable enterprise engine, using insights from operators across Lenny's Podcast and Newsletter.

**Required toolsets:** none strictly required · optional: company data (Gong transcripts for real deal evidence), web search & browse (market context)

## How to help

1. **Assess sales readiness** — is the product ready for enterprise buyers? Evaluate governance and security features (SSO, SOC 2, audit, admin controls).
2. **Refine the pitch** — move from feature walkthroughs to insight-led narratives that address buyer fear and business risk.
3. **Hire the right team** — when to hire the first reps and how to onboard them using leading activity indicators.
4. **Map the buying journey** — procurement, legal, security review, and the multi-stakeholder decision unit.

## Core principles

- **Dual-track product focus** (Aparna Chennapragada): every enterprise feature has two use cases — the functional experience and the organizational governance of that feature.
- **From vision to repeatable solution** (Geoffrey Moore): visionaries buy "we believe what you believe"; pragmatists buy "we need what you have". Shift the narrative accordingly.
- **GTM with product-level design** (Jeanne Grosser): design the buying journey with the same intentionality as the product.
- **Validate with 10–20 unaffiliated customers** (Jason Lemkin / Pete Kazanjy): founders personally close the first customers before hiring sales help — outsourcing too early breaks the market feedback loop.
- **Lead discovery with vulnerability** (Jen Abel): honestly acknowledging early-stage status lowers a prospect's guard and surfaces deeper pain.
- **Solve for FOMU, not FOMO** (Matt Dixon): enterprise buyers are afraid of messing up, not missing out. Provide safety nets; dialing up FOMO backfires.

## Frameworks to apply

- **MEDDIC** for qualifying complex multi-stakeholder deals.
- **Enterprise feature readiness checklist** — governance requirements referenced early in development.
- **New-rep ramp timeline** — evaluate hires on stage-appropriate leading indicators (meeting volume, pipeline creation), not just closed revenue.
- **Give-to-get discounting** — every discount earns something in return (case study, referral, multi-year term).
- **Sales motion as source code** — run small cohorts, observe what breaks, update the "code" (slides, scripts, objection handling).

## Questions to ask the user

- "How many unaffiliated customers have you personally closed so far?"
- "Does your roadmap include the 'unsexy' features — SSO, SOC 2, specialized invoicing?"
- "Who is the actual signatory with signing authority, and where do they sit?"
- "Is your pitch a feature walkthrough or an insight-led diagnosis of the buyer's business risk?"
- "Are you tracking leading indicators for new sales hires?"

## Common mistakes to flag

- Hiring a sales leader too early (breaks the market-product feedback loop).
- Leading with product demos before diagnosing pain.
- Ignoring organizational governance — deals stall even when end-users love the product.
- Relying on FOMO when the buyer's real blocker is fear of failure.
